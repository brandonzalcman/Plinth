# Plinth 0.2.2

Plinth now keeps your tabs where they are when you have lots of them open, stops the window from flashing when you drag it, and moves logins between Plinth and Apple Passwords.

## Highlights

- **A steadier bar with many tabs.** When your tabs no longer fit, every tab's name is shortened to the same width. If they still don't fit, the row of tabs scrolls, and a tile at the end shows how many pages are open and lists every one of them. Selecting a tab only highlights it: no other tab moves or changes size, and the bar scrolls only when the tab you picked would be out of sight. Sites no longer fold up on their own to make room; a site you fold yourself still does.
- **Apple Passwords.** Settings › Passwords has a new Apple Passwords section. Bring your logins into Plinth from the Passwords app's export, and Plinth can move the export file to the Trash afterwards, since it holds your passwords as plain text. Or export the logins you've saved in Plinth as a file the Passwords app can import. Plinth remembers what it has already sent and offers only new or changed logins next time.

## Fixes

- Dragging a window no longer makes the screen flash.
- A page left open for a while no longer turns blue. If a page's web content stops (for example when macOS reclaims memory), Plinth reloads it straight away behind a picture of the page, and pages in the background reload when you next open them.

## Good to know

- macOS doesn't let browsers other than Safari fill from Apple Passwords or save into it, so Plinth keeps its own logins in your Mac's Keychain, and you move logins across by importing or exporting a file. Settings › Passwords says this too.
- The file you export for the Passwords app holds your passwords as plain text. Delete it once you've imported it.

Requires macOS 26. The AI features need a Mac with Apple Intelligence turned on.
